Plank Master Guide

Build core strength, improve posture, and develop a stronger, more stable body.

🟢 Beginner Planks

For those starting their core training

  • Wall Planks: Place hands on wall at shoulder height, step feet back. 3 sets of 20-30 seconds.
  • Incline Planks: Hands on a bench or elevated surface. Easier than ground planks. 3 sets of 20-40 seconds.
  • Knee Planks: Start on knees instead of toes. 3 sets of 15-30 seconds.
  • Forearm Planks: Standard forearm plank position. 3 sets of 20-45 seconds.
  • Rest: 60-90 seconds between sets

🟡 Intermediate Planks

Build strength and endurance

  • Standard Plank: Full plank on hands and toes. 4 sets of 45-90 seconds.
  • Side Planks: One arm extended, targets obliques. 3 sets of 30-60 seconds per side.
  • Plank with Leg Lift: Standard plank, lift legs alternately. 3 sets of 30 seconds.
  • Plank with Shoulder Taps: Tap opposite shoulder while planking. 3 sets of 30 seconds.
  • Rest: 45-60 seconds between sets

🔴 Advanced Plank Variations

Challenge yourself with expert-level variations

  • Plank Jacks: Jump feet in and out while holding plank. 3 sets of 20-30 reps.
  • Decline Plank: Feet elevated on bench or chair. 3 sets of 60-90 seconds.
  • One-Arm Plank: Hold plank with one arm, other extended. 3 sets of 30-45 seconds per side.
  • Archer Plank: Shift weight side to side in plank position. 3 sets of 40 seconds.
  • Plank Push-Ups: Move from forearm to hand plank. 3 sets of 10-15 reps.
  • Rest: 30-45 seconds between sets

📖 How to Do a Proper Plank

  1. Start in a forearm plank position: Place your forearms on the ground with elbows directly under your shoulders. Extend your legs behind you, balancing on your toes.
  2. Align your body: Create a straight line from your head to your heels. Avoid letting your hips sag or pike upward.
  3. Engage your core: Tighten your abdominal muscles and glutes. This keeps your body rigid and prevents injury.
  4. Position your head: Keep your head neutral, looking down at the ground slightly in front of you. Avoid straining your neck.
  5. Focus on breathing: Breathe steadily throughout the hold. Don't hold your breath; keep your breathing regular and controlled.
  6. Hold steady: Maintain the position for as long as you can while keeping proper form. Quality over quantity!
  7. Rest and repeat: Take breaks between sets and gradually increase your hold time as you build strength.

Tips for Perfecting Your Plank

  • Ensure your body forms a straight line from head to heels—no sagging or piking.
  • Keep your elbows directly under your shoulders for proper alignment.
  • Engage your core and glutes throughout the entire hold.
  • Start with shorter holds (15-30 seconds) and gradually increase duration.
  • If the standard plank is too difficult, begin with knee planks or incline planks.
  • Keep your neck neutral—don't strain by looking up.
  • Use a mirror to check your form if possible.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Sagging hips: Keep hips level with shoulders, not drooping below.
  • Piked hips: Avoid lifting hips too high; maintain straight alignment.
  • Poor elbow placement: Elbows should be directly under shoulders, not in front or behind.
  • Neck strain: Don't look forward or up; keep head neutral.
  • Inconsistent breathing: Don't hold your breath; breathe steadily throughout.
  • Overtraining: Don't overdo it; quality form is more important than duration.
  • Ignoring pain: Stop if you feel sharp pain; muscle fatigue is normal, pain is not.
